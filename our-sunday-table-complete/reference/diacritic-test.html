Àmì Ohùn — Rendering Test

Checking whether the brand typefaces can set the collection names correctly before anything goes into design or print.
The hard case is Olúmọ̀. The ọ needs a dot below (U+1ECD) and a grave accent above (U+0300) — two marks stacked on one letter. Many typefaces render the dot but drop or misplace the tone mark, or silently substitute a fallback glyph that doesn't match the surrounding text. Compare the ọ̀ below against the plain o beside it: the dot should sit centred beneath, the grave centred above, and the letterform should match its neighbours exactly.

What to look for

  1. Does ọ̀ show both marks — dot below and grave above?
  2. Is the grave centred over the letter, not shoved right or clipped?
  3. Does the ọ̀ letterform match the o next to it, or has a different font substituted in?
  4. Do the marks survive at small sizes and in the uppercase nav label?
  5. Does the italic hold up — italics are where stacked marks usually break first?
