Why your cakes sink.

Three causes account for almost every sunken cake. Watch the film, then keep the notes.

Play the lesson
Lesson 01 · Troubleshooting6 min 12 sec

A cake sinks because the structure set too late, or never set at all. Everything below is a version of that one problem.

1. You opened the oven too early

Before the proteins and starches set, your cake is a foam held up by hot air. Cold air hits it, the gas contracts, and the middle drops. The tell is a wide, shallow dip across the whole top. Don't open the door for the first two-thirds of the bake.

2. Too much leavening

Counterintuitive, but more baking powder means bigger bubbles, and bigger bubbles are weaker. They inflate fast, merge, and collapse before the crumb can set around them. The tell is a deep central crater and a coarse, tunnelled crumb. Roughly 1 teaspoon per 120g of flour, measured level.

3. Underbaked, plainly

The edges set, the centre didn't, and it fell on the cooling rack. The tell is a dense, slightly wet streak at the bottom of the dip. A skewer should come out with a few moist crumbs — clean often means overbaked.

The Science

Setting is a race

Egg proteins coagulate around 70°C and starch gelatinises around 80°C — together they turn foam into cake. Leavening peaks well before that. If the gas is spent before 80°C, there is nothing holding the roof up when it cools.
